# Anatomy & Physiology C — Event Breakdown

## Overview
- **Team size:** Up to 2 participants
- **Duration:** ~50 minutes
- **Format:** Either a written test OR a series of lab-practical stations (decided by the Event Supervisor, not the competitors)
- **Calculator:** Class II (non-programmable, non-graphing) — up to 2 per team
- **Allowed resource:** One (1) sheet of paper, 8.5" x 11", information on both sides, any content, any source. May be laminated or in a sheet protector for durability. Affixed labels and multiple sheets (even inside one protector) are NOT allowed.
- **Scoring:** High score wins. Ties broken by pre-selected questions chosen in advance by the Event Supervisor.

## Content Scope (fixed for this competition cycle)
The event tests three body systems. Depth of content increases by tournament tier — Regional < State < National, with State/National-only and National-only subtopics explicitly marked.

### 1. Nervous System
- **All levels:**
  - Cellular anatomy/physiology of neurons: synapses, neurotransmitters, glial cell types and functions
  - Electrophysiology: qualitative understanding of action potentials in myelinated vs. unmyelinated neurons; qualitative AND quantitative understanding of membrane potential models (Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz equation)
  - CNS: detailed cerebrum structure/function (cognition, sensation, movement, memory, language); general overview of cerebellum/brainstem; general spinal cord/nerve root organization
  - Identification of simple EEG waveforms
  - Peripheral nervous system: neural ganglia, sensory/motor neuron physiology, sympathetic vs. parasympathetic organization, reflex arc physiology
  - Named disorders (with mechanism, not just name): Epilepsy, Alzheimer's, Multiple Sclerosis, Parkinson's, Cerebral Palsy, Stroke, ALS
  - Drug effects on the nervous system: alcohol, caffeine, nicotine, marijuana
  - Toxin effects (specific toxins only): tetrodotoxin, curare, botulinum toxin, anatoxin-a, tetanus toxin
- **State & National only:**
  - Detailed brainstem/cerebellum structure including specific nuclei and tracts
  - Cranial nerve pathways, classification, clinical assessment
  - Detailed spinal cord structure including specific ascending/descending tracts
  - General senses (touch, pressure, pain, temperature, itch, proprioception) and their spinal cord/PNS relation
- **National only:**
  - Cable theory model of the neuron (modeling as an RC circuit)
  - Quantitative action potential models (e.g., Hodgkin-Huxley model, spiking neuron models)
  - Detailed cerebral vasculature (internal carotid, vertebral, basilar arteries, Circle of Willis, ACA/MCA/PCA/SCA/AICA/PICA)
  - Treatments/prevention for all nervous system conditions listed above

### 2. Special Senses
- **All levels:**
  - Sight: eye structures (cornea, lens, iris, pupil, retina, optic nerve) and function; how light is focused; rod/cone function
  - Hearing/balance: ear structures (outer/middle/inner ear) and function; how cochlea/hair cells detect frequency/amplitude; vestibular system and balance
  - Smell: nasal cavity, olfactory epithelium, receptor cells, olfactory bulb; odor binding mechanism
  - Taste: tongue, taste buds, papillae, receptor cells, gustatory nerves
  - Disorders: myopia, hyperopia, presbyopia, nyctalopia, astigmatism, conjunctivitis, color blindness, otitis externa/media, types of deafness, anosmia/dysosmia, dysgeusia
- **State & National only:**
  - Visual pathway: retina → optic nerve → optic chiasm → optic tract → LGN → visual cortex
  - Visual field defects from damage at different pathway points (monocular blindness, bitemporal hemianopia, homonymous hemianopia)
- **National only:**
  - Pupillary light reflex circuit (pretectal nucleus, Edinger-Westphal nucleus, oculomotor nerve); miosis via parasympathetic, mydriasis via sympathetic
  - Diabetic Retinopathy, Macular Degeneration, Glaucoma, Otosclerosis, Presbycusis, Meniere's Disease
  - Treatments/prevention for all conditions above

### 3. Endocrine System
- **All levels:**
  - Three hormone classes: steroids, peptides, amines — compare/contrast
  - Mechanisms of hormone action: nuclear vs. cytoplasmic
  - Endocrine problems: hypersecretion, hyposecretion, hypersensitivity, hyposensitivity
  - Hormones and functions by gland: hypothalamus, pituitary, pineal, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, adrenal glands, pancreas, gonads
  - Disorders: diabetes mellitus, central diabetes insipidus, hypoglycemia, hyperparathyroidism, hypoparathyroidism, hyperthyroidism (Graves'), hypothyroidism (Hashimoto's), goiter, congenital iodine deficiency, primary hyperaldosteronism, PCOS
- **State & National only:**
  - Hormones from additional organs: liver, kidneys, heart, adipose tissue
  - Endocrine disruptors: DDT, BPA, PFAS (exposure routes and effects)
  - Additional disorders: Cushing's Syndrome, Addison's Disease, Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumors
  - Endocrine cycles, negative feedback, autonomic control of endocrine function
- **National only:**
  - Endocrine system's role in the stress response
  - Additional disorders: Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia, Myxedema, acromegaly, pituitary dwarfism
  - Treatments/prevention for all conditions above

## What Actually Happens on Competition Day

1. **Check-in:** The team of up to 2 arrives at their assigned room/time slot. Each participant may carry in writing utensils, up to 2 Class II calculators, and their single double-sided cheat sheet.

2. **Format determination:** The Event Supervisor has pre-decided whether this run of the event is a **written test** or a **lab-practical station rotation**. Competitors don't choose this.

3a. **If written test format:** The team receives one test packet and works through it together for the full ~50 minutes. Question types may include multiple choice, short answer, diagram labeling, graph/data interpretation, and calculations (hence the calculator allowance — e.g., using the Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz equation).

3b. **If lab-practical station format:** The room is divided into numbered stations. Each station presents something physical or visual — a specimen, anatomical model, microscope slide, illustration, or a data set/graph — paired with a question or set of questions. Teams rotate through stations on a supervisor-set timer, answering as they go.

4. **Answering:** Regardless of format, questions draw only from the content scope above, filtered to the appropriate tournament level (Regional/State/National) — so a Regional-level test will not include National-only content like cable theory or cerebral vasculature.

5. **Submission:** The team turns in their completed packet or station answer sheet at the end of the time period. There is no equipment to impound, no physical device to return, and no appeal process tied to hardware — the appeal process (if used) concerns re-grading of answers, not device compliance.

6. **Scoring:** Each question has a pre-assigned point value. The team's total determines their rank — highest score wins. If two or more teams are tied, the Event Supervisor uses a pre-selected set of tiebreaker questions (chosen before the competition, not on the fly) to determine final placement.

## Key Competitive Realities
- The one-page cheat sheet is a **support tool for dense, list-like recall** (hormone tables, toxin names, precise gland-hormone pairings) — it does not substitute for conceptual understanding, since many questions require explaining *mechanisms* (why a disorder causes its symptoms) rather than just naming facts.
- Lab-practical stations can appear with **no advance warning of which specimens/models** will be used, so recognition skill (not just factual recall) matters.
- Time pressure is real: ~50 minutes across three full body systems (or per-station time limits) means pacing matters as much as raw knowledge.

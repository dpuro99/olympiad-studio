# Boomilever C — Event Breakdown

## Overview
- **Team size:** Up to 2 participants
- **Duration:** ~10 minutes (competition/testing time)
- **Format:** Build event — teams design and construct a wooden cantilevered structure BEFORE the tournament, then bring it to be tested on-site
- **Eye Protection:** Category B required during testing (ANSI Z87+ marking)
- **Device:** Only one structure per team may be entered; it must be built prior to competition (no on-site construction)

## What the Device Must Do
A Boomilever is a cantilevered beam or truss structure that:
- Attaches to a vertical **Testing Wall** by resting on a **Mounting Bolt Assembly**
- Extends outward from the wall and supports a hanging **Loading Assembly** (a bucket that gets filled with sand) at a specified horizontal distance from the wall
- Must hold as much load as possible relative to its own mass — **efficiency, not raw strength, is what's scored**

## Construction Parameters (must all be true for a legal, Tier 1 structure)
- **Single structure**, no separate/loose/detachable parts
- Built **only from wood**, bonded **only by adhesive** — no other materials permitted anywhere
  - *Wood* = the hard fibrous substance from stems/branches/trunks/roots beneath the bark. Explicitly excludes: bark, particleboard, wood composites, bamboo/grasses, paper, plywood (commercially laminated wood), sawdust/shaving-and-adhesive composites. Wood cannot be painted, soaked, chemically modified, color-enhanced, or have tape/labels affixed (ink barcodes/process markings are OK).
  - *Lamination* (gluing layers together by the team) is allowed without restriction; *commercially* laminated wood (plywood) is not.
  - *Adhesive* = any commercially available glue/cement/cyanoacrylate/epoxy/hot melt/polyurethane/super glue. **Adhesive tapes are NOT allowed.**
- No cross-sectional size limits on individual wood pieces
- Structure must contact the Testing Wall **only via the Mounting Bolt Assembly** (a bolt sticking out of the wall that the structure rests on)
- Structure must support the Loading Assembly's chain such that the chain centerline is:
  - At least 40 cm but no more than 45 cm horizontally from the Testing Wall
  - No more than 2.5 cm horizontally off-center from the wall's centerline
- During testing, the structure may **only** touch the wall **outside** the marked Vertical Contact Width Lines (4 cm to either side of the mounting bolt centerline)
- **Two scoring options** (team picks which to build for):
  - **Base Option:** Structure may only touch the wall between the 10 cm and 15 cm Horizontal Contact Depth Lines, AND the Loading Block's bottom must sit above the 15 cm depth line before loading
  - **Load Scored BONUS Option:** Structure may only touch the wall above the 10 cm depth line, AND the Loading Block's bottom must sit above the 10 cm depth line, AND **the structure must hold at least 15 kg** — if it meets all three, the team gets a flat +5,000 g added to their Load Scored (a huge scoring advantage, but a much stricter build requirement)

## Test Apparatus (provided by Event Supervisor, NOT the team)
- **Testing Wall:** rigid plywood surface, ≥40 cm wide × 30 cm high, marked with:
  - A Mounting Bolt hole (≤0.266" diameter) ~5 cm below the top, centered
  - Horizontal Contact Depth Lines at 10 cm, 15 cm, and 20 cm below the bolt
  - Vertical Contact Width Lines 4 cm to either side of the bolt
- **Mounting Bolt Assembly:** a ¼-20 bolt (2.36" long) with hex nuts and washers, installed to protrude 3.0 cm (±0.1 cm) from the wall face — this is what the structure rests on
- **Loading Assembly:** a 5 cm × 5 cm × ~2 cm Loading Block on a threaded eyebolt + wing nut, connected via chain and S-hook to a 5-gallon bucket. Total mass of this assembly (block + eyebolt + chain + bucket, empty) is capped at 1,500 g
- **Sand:** clean, dry, free-flowing material (like play sand) — this is what's poured into the bucket to apply load
- **Bucket Stabilizing Sticks:** two dowels with spring-tip doorstops, used by participants to keep the swinging bucket from moving side to side (NOT to help support the load)
- **Structure Scale:** digital scale for weighing the structure itself (before loading), ideally to 0.01 g precision
- **Sand/Load Scale:** separate digital scale for weighing the sand + Loading Assembly to verify final load supported

## What Actually Happens on Competition Day

1. **Pre-competition setup (done by Event Supervisor, not teams):** The wall is set up and leveled, the Mounting Bolt Assembly is installed and tightened, contact lines are marked, and the combined mass of the Loading Assembly + sand is verified to be at least 15,100 g and no more than 15,200 g available for use.

2. **Check-in:** Team arrives with proper eye protection (Category B / Z87+) and their pre-built structure. Once check-in begins, they may **not** leave, get outside help, or receive new materials until testing is complete, and **no alterations to the structure are allowed** past this point.

3. **Inspection at check-in:**
   - Supervisor verifies eye protection
   - Supervisor inspects the structure for construction/material compliance
   - Team submits their **Estimated Load Supported** on paper — this number is used later as a tiebreaker
   - Structure is placed on the Structure Scale and its mass is recorded

4. **Testing window (6 minutes):**
   - Team sets up their structure on the Testing Wall, resting it on the Mounting Bolt Assembly
   - They attach the Loading Assembly (chain + bucket), reassembling it in the exact order the supervisor gave them if they had to take it apart to install it
   - The bucket must have floor clearance for tilting or for the structure to deflect under load
   - Supervisor checks positioning/contact-zone compliance and confirms which scoring option (Base vs. Bonus) the structure qualifies for
   - **Sand pouring begins:** team members pour sand into the bucket by hand, only allowed to steady the bucket using the tips of the Bucket Stabilizing Sticks (no touching the structure itself once loading starts)
   - Pouring continues until: the structure **fails** (breaks/deforms/can no longer hold the load), **something other than the structure starts supporting the load**, or **time runs out** (6 minutes)
   - If pouring continued briefly past failure/time by mistake, the supervisor may remove excess sand at their discretion

5. **Measurement:** The combined mass of the Loading Assembly + sand actually poured in (minus any structure pieces that fell into the bucket) is weighed and recorded to the nearest gram. This is the **Load Supported**.
   - Minimum Load Supported = the empty Loading Assembly's mass (if the structure can't even hold that, it's placed in Tier 3, "unable to be loaded")
   - Maximum Load Supported is capped at 15,000 g even if more sand could theoretically be added

6. **Data review & sign-off:** The supervisor reviews the recorded data with the team, and once acknowledged, testing is officially complete. If a team wants to file an appeal, they must leave their structure with the supervisor (it can't be taken home immediately).

7. **Design knowledge check:** At some point (per General Rules), participants must be able to answer questions from the supervisor about their structure's design, construction, and operation — this isn't a separate timed portion but is a standing requirement of the event.

## Scoring
- **High score wins.**
- **Score = Load Scored ÷ Structure Mass** (i.e., load-to-weight efficiency ratio, not raw load)
- **Load Scored = Load Supported (measured) + Load Scored Bonus** (either 0 or +5,000 g depending on whether the team met the stricter Bonus construction option AND held 15 kg)
- **Tiers:**
  - **Tier 1:** Meets all construction and competition requirements
  - **Tier 2:** Fails to meet one or more construction/competition requirements (still gets scored, just ranked below Tier 1)
  - **Tier 3:** Structure couldn't be loaded at all, didn't support minimum load, or team lacked eye protection — ranked purely by structure mass (lightest to heaviest) within this tier
- **Tiebreakers (in order):**
  1. Whose Estimated Load Supported (submitted at check-in) was closest to their actual Load Supported
  2. Lowest structure mass

## Key Competitive Realities
- This is fundamentally an **optimization problem**: maximize (load held + bonus) while minimizing structure mass — a heavier structure that holds more isn't necessarily better if the ratio is worse.
- The **Bonus Option is high-risk/high-reward**: it requires touching the wall in a stricter zone AND guaranteeing at least 15 kg of holding capacity, but rewards a flat +5,000 g to the numerator, which can massively swing the score if achieved (and costs nothing extra if the structure was going to hold 15+ kg anyway).
- Material constraints are strict and specifically exclude common "clever" materials (bamboo, plywood, tape) — only true wood + non-tape adhesive.
- **Pre-tournament data logging matters**: teams should be testing prototypes at home/school (their own scale, their own load simulation) to refine designs before the one-shot 6-minute official test, since there's no do-over.
- Estimating your own Load Supported accurately (for the tiebreaker) rewards teams who've built enough prototypes to *predict* failure load, not just hope for a good one.

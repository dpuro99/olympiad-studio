# Machines C — Event Breakdown

## Overview
- **Team size:** Up to 2 participants
- **Duration:** ~50 minutes (Part I written test minimum 25 minutes; Part II device testing is separately timed)
- **Format:** Two-part event — Part I is a written test, Part II is a pre-built device measurement task
- **Calculator:** Class III — each team may bring two

## Event Parameters
- Each team may bring a collection of notes/resources (any size binder, notebooks, folders, sheet protectors, lamination, tabs, labels all permitted) — same permissive format as Circuit Lab
- Each team may bring non-electronic tools and supplies, writing utensils, and two Class III calculators
- Each team may bring **one pre-constructed device** (built before the tournament — this is a build component embedded within an otherwise "knowledge test" style event)
- The Event Supervisor provides all testing materials (the actual test masses) — teams should NOT bring their own masses

## What the Device Must Do
Teams build (in advance) a **compound lever device** — essentially a measuring instrument — capable of determining the **mass ratios** between three unknown test masses (labeled A, B, C) provided by the Event Supervisor on competition day, without ever being told the actual masses.

## Construction Parameters
- The device must be a **Class 1 lever connected directly (via a flexible or rigid link) to a Class 2 or 3 lever** — i.e., two different lever classes chained together
- Each lever has a single "beam" (the bar resting on the fulcrum) with a maximum length of **40.0 cm**, measured without the test masses attached
- **Springs, electric components, and electronic components are prohibited**
- The device must be built to accommodate the Event Supervisor's test masses (which use a flexible loop/hook attachment)
- Participants may NOT bring their own masses or attach fixed masses to the device on-site except when fixing them in place *before* testing to achieve static equilibrium; lightweight adjustable hooks used solely to hold the test masses are allowed and don't need to be fixed

## Test Masses (provided by Event Supervisor, hidden from teams)
- Three masses labeled A, B, and C, each with a flexible loop (large enough for a golf ball to pass through) tied to the top
- Each mass (including loop) must fit inside a 15×15×20 cm box, and weighs between 20g and 800g
- The ratio of heaviest-to-lightest mass is capped: 8.0 at Regionals, 10.0 at States, 12.0 at Nationals
- Multiple testing stations may be used, but the mass ratios at each station are identical (though actual mass values may differ slightly) — this ensures fairness across teams tested at different stations

## What Actually Happens on Competition Day

### Part I: Written Test (minimum 25 minutes)
Covers simple and compound machines in **static equilibrium** (and, at State/Nationals, machines that are **not** in equilibrium), including:
- Ideal vs. actual mechanical advantage
- Efficiency, load, and effort
- Potential and kinetic energy
- Coefficient of friction
- Self-locking machines
- Angle of repose
- **State/National only:** simple/compound machines that are not in equilibrium (dynamic scenarios)

At least 6 of these simple machine types must appear in questions: first/second/third class levers, inclined planes, wedges, wheel and axle (including gears), pulleys (including rope/pulley systems), and screws.

Questions may also draw on general Newtonian mechanics (motion, inertia, force, action-reaction, speed/velocity/acceleration, momentum, energy conservation) and require:
- Basic 2D geometry (parallel/perpendicular lines, triangle relationships)
- Multi-step algebraic manipulation
- Basic trigonometry and vectors
- **No calculus** is required or allowed to be necessary

### Part II: Device Testing
1. **While the rest of the team works on Part I**, the Event Supervisor individually calls each team (or one team member) aside for **Device Setup and Testing** — this may happen at the same table or a separate station.

2. **Setup Time** (5 minutes at Regionals, 4 minutes at State/Nationals): includes unpacking, assembly, and calibration. The Supervisor checks the device against construction specs once assembled and tells the team immediately if something's out of spec.

3. **Bringing device into compliance:** If the beam is too long, the team can mark an "allowed beam length" with supervisor-provided tape and use only that portion — no penalty, but no extra time either. If movable masses were brought (a violation), they can be taped in place without penalty. No additional setup time is granted for these fixes.

4. **Device Test Time** (4 minutes, minus any overage used correcting Setup issues): Teams may NOT touch the actual test masses until the Supervisor hands them over — and won't receive them until the device is confirmed in spec. Using **only** the physical principles of the lever (adjusting relative positions of masses and/or fulcrums along the beams), the team must determine the **mass ratios** between the test masses. They may work with two or three masses at a time, and may use their notes/resources/calculators to do so.

5. **Submitting answers:** Teams submit mass ratios in **decimal form** (e.g., a 5:2 ratio is submitted as "2.5"), to a precision specified by the Event Supervisor. The Supervisor also specifies what action "locks in" the answer (writing it down, announcing it's final, a bell, etc.) — timing stops at that action.

6. **Constraint:** Teams must not mark, attach anything to, or modify the test masses themselves.

7. **Timing:** Part II timing stops when the team submits both required ratios (A/B and B/C) or when the 4-minute Device Test time runs out — whichever comes first, recorded to the nearest tenth of a second.

## Scoring
- **High score wins. Final Score = Exam Score (ES) + Ratio Score 1 (R1) + Ratio Score 2 (R2) + Time Score (TS)**, capped at 100 total points
- **Exam Score (ES)** = (team's Part I score ÷ highest Part I score among all teams) × 45 points — this is **relative** scoring, not absolute
- **Time Score (TS)** = ((240 − team's Part II time in seconds) ÷ 240) × 15 points — faster device measurement = more points
- **Ratio Scores (R1 for A/B, R2 for B/C)** = (1 − |Actual Ratio − Measured Ratio| ÷ Actual Ratio) × 20 points each — accuracy-based, closer to the true value = more points, floor of 0
- **Penalty:** If a team violates a COMPETITION rule (as opposed to construction), their TS, R1, and R2 are all multiplied by 0.9
- **Teams with no device, no ratio estimate, a device that can't meet spec, or that don't make an honest attempt** receive R1=R2=TS=0 but are still allowed to compete on Part I (the written exam)
- **Tiebreakers (in order):** best Exam Score → best Time Score → best R1 → selected test questions

## Key Competitive Realities
- **This event splits its scoring almost evenly between pure knowledge (45%) and a live measurement skill (55% combined)** — meaning strong written-test prep alone caps out well below a winning score; the physical device performance matters just as much or more.
- **The device must be built and calibrated in advance without knowing the actual masses or even the exact ratio limit for the competition level** — teams need a design that generalizes well across a range of possible mass ratios (up to 8-12x depending on level) rather than one tuned to a single expected scenario.
- **Speed and accuracy are in tension and both separately scored** — the Time Score rewards fast measurement while R1/R2 reward precise measurement, so a well-designed device and a well-drilled measurement *procedure* (not just device quality) both matter; teams that fumble the process lose time even with a great device.
- **The Exam Score is normalized against the field's best score, not an absolute scale** — meaning Part I performance is inherently competitive/relative, so understanding how difficult the exam is likely to be relative to other teams changes how much weight to put on device performance vs. written prep.
- **Static equilibrium (lever balance) is the exam's home turf and also directly what the device physically demonstrates** — meaning strong theoretical understanding of levers, mechanical advantage, and equilibrium conditions reinforces both halves of the event simultaneously, making this a high-leverage area to master deeply rather than splitting attention evenly across all six machine types.
- **State/Nationals adds non-equilibrium dynamics questions** to the written exam that Regional-level prep won't cover — a distinct, harder theoretical jump beyond the mostly-static-equilibrium focus at Regionals.

# Rocks and Minerals C — 2027 Rules Summary

**Status:** Verified against the supplied Division C Rules Manual, pp. C52–C56 (PDF pp. 57–61), checked October 8, 2026.

## Format and allowed resources
- Team of up to 2; approximately 50 minutes; Class II calculator.
- One magnifying glass; one binder of any size, one commercial field guide, and one copy of the 2027 National Rocks and Minerals List. During specimen/display stations, only the official list may be removed from the binder. Teams may not bring specimens.
- Task-oriented exam/stations can include IDs, observation, data, graphs and charts. If observation is insufficient, diagnostic properties are provided. HCl is not used/provided; no taste tests.

## Scope
Identification is 30–50% of points and questions are balanced between rocks and minerals. Identification is limited to the official 2027 list; other material may illustrate concepts.
- **Minerals:** definition; physical/other properties; limited mineral habits; formula/property relationships; specified solid-solution series and two-component phase diagrams; polymorphs; groups, silicate structures and crystal systems; formation/environments; Bowen’s series; uses and listed gemstones.
- **Rocks:** definitions and classification; igneous texture/composition/formations/plate settings/melt evolution; sedimentary texture, grain properties, environments and structures; metamorphic textures, minerals, protoliths, grades/facies and tectonic settings; rock cycle and uses.
- State/National: incompatible elements and pegmatites; thin-section photomicrographs with explicit mineral and texture limits. Crystal systems are listed in the general mineral scope (not restricted to State/National).

The official specimen list and notes control item names/levels. Its footnotes include starred State/National specimens, diagnostic data, accepted feldspar/tourmaline naming, Bauxite classification and Rock Salt/Gypsum equivalences.

## Scoring
High score wins. Question/section point values may be specified; preselected questions/sections break ties.

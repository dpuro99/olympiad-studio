# Dynamic Planet C — Oceanography — Event Breakdown

## Overview
- **Team size:** Up to 2 participants
- **Duration:** ~50 minutes
- **Format:** Exam and/or timed stations (Event Supervisor's choice)
- **Calculator:** Class II — each team may bring two
- **Allowed resource:** One binder of **any size** containing information in any form from any source, attached via the binder's rings. Sheet protectors, lamination, tabs, and labels are all permitted (much more permissive than the single-sheet events).
- **Special restriction:** If the event uses a rotation through laboratory stations (interacting with samples/displays), **no material may be removed from the binder** during the event.

## Content Scope
This year's Dynamic Planet focuses specifically on **physical and geological oceanography**:

### 1. Seawater Properties & Distribution
- Temperature (thermocline, sea surface temperature)
- Salinity (water cycle effects, salt sources)
- Pressure & density (three-layer ocean model, stratification, mixing)
- Other properties: nutrient concentrations, pH, chlorophyll

### 2. Surface Circulation
- Warm vs. cool currents
- Coriolis effect
- Surface convergence and divergence
- Western intensification
- Ekman transport
- Eddies

### 3. Large-Scale Circulation
- Thermohaline circulation
- Global ocean energy transport
- Water masses and fronts
- Deep water formation

### 4. Waves
- Wave types (wind waves, tsunamis, storm surge) and how they form
- Wave interaction with coasts (surf, wave shoaling)
- Related properties: wave velocity, wave height, wavelength, period, fetch

### 5. Tides
- Diurnal/semidiurnal/mixed tidal patterns
- Spring/neap tides
- Resonance
- Tidal currents

### 6. Coastal Features and Processes
- Estuaries
- Coastal erosional and depositional landforms
- Coral reef and atoll formation
- Longshore currents, rip currents
- Coastal upwelling and downwelling

### 7. Geological Oceanography
- Tectonic processes in ocean basins (seafloor spreading and its evidence, oceanic crust composition/evolution)
- Ocean floor features: ridges, seamounts, trenches, continental rise/slope/shelf
- Oceanic sediment: sediment types, turbidity currents/turbidites, abyssal plains

### 8. Climate Variability and Change
- Atmosphere/ocean variability: El Niño Southern Oscillation, Pacific Decadal Oscillation, Southern Annular Mode
- Ocean climate change effects: sea level rise, thermal expansion, coastal flooding, acidification, deoxygenation

### 9. Oceanic Research Tools
- Water samples, sediment cores, seawater velocity trackers, sonar/acoustic sensing, CTD devices

### 10. Physics of Ocean Circulation
- Pressure-gradient force, geostrophic balance, Rossby waves, Kelvin waves

## What Actually Happens on Competition Day

1. **Check-in:** Team arrives with their (any-size) binder and up to 2 Class II calculators. Because there's no single-sheet limit, teams typically bring a substantial reference binder.

2. **Format determination:** The Event Supervisor decides whether the competition runs as a straight written exam, a series of timed stations, or a mix of both.

3a. **If exam-only format:** Team works through a written packet covering the topic areas above, likely including calculation-based questions (e.g., wave period/wavelength relationships, tidal current timing, Coriolis-driven current direction, geostrophic balance problems).

3b. **If station-based format:** The team rotates through stations, each likely tied to a specific sub-topic — e.g., a station with real bathymetric/ocean floor data to interpret, a station with a graph of ENSO indices, a station showing sediment core data, or a station with oceanographic instrument diagrams to identify/explain. **Critically, once stations begin, no material can be pulled out of the binder** — so the binder needs to be internally organized and quickly navigable, since flipping through pages under a station time limit is itself a skill.

4. **Working through content:** Given the breadth of topics (10 major categories spanning seawater chemistry, circulation at multiple scales, waves, tides, coastal geomorphology, plate tectonics as it relates to oceans, climate variability, and even basic fluid dynamics like Rossby/Kelvin waves), questions will likely mix:
   - Conceptual explanation (e.g., "explain why Ekman transport causes X")
   - Data/graph interpretation (e.g., reading a thermocline profile, an ENSO index chart, or a bathymetric cross-section)
   - Calculations (e.g., wave speed from period and wavelength, tidal range predictions, or geostrophic velocity estimates)
   - Identification (e.g., naming an ocean floor feature from a cross-section, or an oceanographic tool from an image)

5. **Submission:** Team completes and turns in their written responses (either as one exam or as station answer sheets).

## Scoring
- **High score wins.**
- Points awarded for quality and accuracy of responses
- **Tiebreakers:** accuracy and/or quality of answers to selected (pre-chosen) questions

## Key Competitive Realities
- **This is one of the more binder-permissive events** (any size, any content, any source) — meaning raw memorization matters less than in single-sheet events, but this shifts the real bottleneck to **binder organization and retrieval speed**, especially in a timed-station format where you can't remove pages mid-event to reorganize on the fly.
- **The topic breadth is genuinely large** — physical oceanography (circulation, waves, tides), geological oceanography (plate tectonics, sediment), AND climate science (ENSO, ocean acidification) are all in scope simultaneously, so a binder needs comprehensive coverage across all of it, not just deep coverage of one area.
- **Quantitative/physics-based sub-skills are a distinct challenge** — topics like geostrophic balance, Rossby/Kelvin waves, and pressure-gradient force require actual physics reasoning (not just recall), and these are exactly the kind of content that's hard to "look up" quickly in a binder under time pressure — so genuine understanding (not just a good reference binder) matters more here than in more recall-heavy events.
- **Named real-world phenomena and objects appear directly in the rules** (specific named features/processes like ENSO, PDO, Southern Annular Mode) — meaning there's a discrete list of "must-know" named phenomena that's very study-able and likely to show up.
- **Station-based competition format tests something binder content alone can't fix**: interpreting novel real data (a graph, a core sample, a bathymetric profile) the team has never seen before requires practiced data-interpretation skill, not just having the right reference page bookmarked.
